1. Context

Wayfinder OS is an AI coordinator that lives in iMessage. You give it a goal and it finds the people, resources and approvals needed to get it done. Along the way it learns how the organization really works. The website is where that work becomes visible.

  • Tagline: Discover. Connect. Coordinate. Evolve.
  • Three engines the site has to show: Coordinate (Wayfinder) splits a goal into routed steps. Discover (Mycelium) matches needs, skills and unused resources, with consent. Evolve (Darwin) replays past workflows and evolves better routing rules that an admin approves.
  • Who sees it: first, judges watching a 1440–1920px screen on stage while phones drive the demo. Later, admins and members looking after their own data.
  • Data: a seeded, simulated company. A complete local demo runs without any API keys and always says when it's using fixtures.

2. North star and guardrails

Frutiger Aero, reimagined as a living operating system from 2040. Picture the hopeful, connected future of the 2000s (Vista Aero, Windows Media Center, early Apple Aqua, the Wii) rebuilt with today's tools. It's a sunlit world made of glass and water, and inside it a bioluminescent network quietly finds, coordinates and improves work.

  • Default mood: bright daytime. Clear cyan sky fading into cerulean ocean and mint ecosystems, with caustics, ripples, iridescent bubbles, brushed white surfaces and thin chrome.
  • Optional mood: deep-ocean night mode. The same world after dark, with the network glowing. This is an alternate theme, not a cyberpunk reskin.
  • Feel: optimistic, intelligent, organic, premium, playful and calm. It should look like a new category of software, not a dashboard.

Never

  • Generic Tailwind admin templates, KPI-tile grids as the main experience, or repeated 3-column feature grids.
  • The default dark-navy gradient with random purple glows, or too much neon.
  • A wall of cards in the hero.
  • Heavy decorative blur, or tiny translucent text.
  • Childish skeuomorphism, stock illustrations, or emoji used as icons.
  • Graphs that are only decoration, buttons that do nothing, or claims about integrations or optimizations that aren't real.

3. Principles

PrincipleWhat it means here
Canvas first, not dashboardEach main route is one living scene with floating glass overlays. Cards hold details; they never make up the scene.
Consent before connectionNo introduction, shared detail or consequential action happens before every relevant person approves. Pending consent is always visible, as an amber ConsentHalo.
Observable, not theatricalWhen a slider moves, a number moves. Every animation stands for a real state change in the data.
One encoding everywhereThe five semantic state colors (section 6) mean the same thing on every page and always come with an icon and a label.
Show where data came fromEvery connection, match and score can be inspected for its source. Simulated or sample data is labelled where it appears.
Beautiful at restThe scene should still look good when nothing is happening. Idle motion stays slow and quiet, and live events take the focus.
Keyboard reachableEvery important action works without a pointer, including on the canvas.

5. Page by page

Welcome /welcome

  • First 5 seconds: a translucent planet-like sphere rises out of a sky-to-ocean gradient. Inside it, hundreds of glowing nodes form and dissolve connections while tiny particles travel along them. A few glass panes float nearby with context.
  • Copy: WAYFINDER OS. "The world is already connected. Now it can collaborate." Supporting copy: "Discover invisible opportunities. Connect complementary people. Coordinate complex goals. Evolve better outcomes."
  • CTAs: primary PearlButton "Enter the Living Network", secondary "Watch a Discovery".
  • Exit: the camera dives into the sphere, and its network becomes the /universe canvas.
  • Must: the title sits on a calm area of the gradient, not on top of busy nodes. Under reduced motion, the scene is a still render with a fade.

Living Universe /universe

  • Scene: a full-viewport network in 2D with some depth, curved Bézier threads, and a seeded, deterministic layout settled with gentle spring physics.
  • Nodes: people, agents, departments, skills, resources, workflows, opportunities and policies, each with its own form (section 6).
  • Interactions: hover shows a short label and lights up the local cluster. Click opens a GlassInspector (connections, activity, permissions, next actions). Double-click focuses the cluster. Scroll and drag pan and zoom.
  • Controls: search, filters by node type and state, legend, minimap, zoom and reset, reduced motion.
  • Rule: a person-to-person thread only appears if its provenance can be shown. Sample data is labelled.

Discovery Garden /discover

  • Concept: a social network with no feed, likes, followers or posts. Instead there's a calm garden.
  • Anatomy: each person has a privacy-controlled AI orb with small tokens orbiting it (skills, interests, needs, resources, preferences). A central Discovery Seed grows strands toward possible clusters. Unconfirmed opportunities are translucent buds. Confirmed ones unfold into a flower-like structure.
  • Pipeline, shown as a step strip: Signal Received → Complementary Match → Opportunity Proposed → Individual Consent → Collaborative Plan → Workflow Launched.
  • Showcase: five distant people (A needs a venue, B has unused event space, C teaches electronics repair, D has broken laptops, E can transport equipment). Their agents discover a community laptop-repair and sustainability workshop that nobody asked for.
  • Controls on every proposal: Approve, Decline, Modify shared info, and Why this match?

AI Agent /agent

  • Assistant orb: a glossy, expressive orb whose inner light shows six states: idle (slow swirl), listening (rings ripple outward), thinking (particles orbit), coordinating (threads reach out to other agents), completed (soft mint bloom), error (coral flicker, then still).
  • Conversation: glass bubbles, interactive step cards, task plans, and a live view of handoffs between agents. Not a fixed chatbot column.
  • Photon iMessage preview: a phone-shaped frame showing sample messages, participants, requests and approvals. Every message carries a label: Simulated, Prepared or Sent. It only says Sent after the backend confirms.
  • Also: a message composer, quick suggestions, workflow approvals, and an event timeline.

Workflow canvas /orchestrate

  • Steps are glass islands. Dependencies are moving, current-like streams.
  • States: active islands have pulsing fluid rings, blocked ones hang as amber droplets, completed ones glow mint.
  • Interactions: drag and drop where it's meaningful, inline controls, task details, dependency inspection, and authorization gates drawn as ConsentHalos.
  • Goal input: a natural-language goal becomes a proposed plan (Gemini when connected, seeded output in demo mode, labelled either way). Nothing consequential runs without approval.

Time Machine /time-machine

  • Layout: layered translucent timelines with two floating panels side by side, Current Reality and Alternative Future.
  • Temporal control: a large circular glass dial that sets staffing, budget, approvals, delays and resources, with linear sliders as the accessible fallback.
  • Outputs: projected duration, cost, handoffs, utilization and bottlenecks, with animated graphs that change when the inputs change.
  • Controls: play, pause, reset, scrub, and scenario comparison. Every figure shows its assumptions, its confidence, and a "Simulated" label.

Digital Darwin /evolution

  • Scene: a garden of evolving strategies. Each strategy is a glowing glass organism (a branching topology) linked into a genealogy tree.
  • Behaviour: generation 0 holds the baselines. With each generation, mutations branch out, weak strategies dim, strong ones brighten, and the top performers carry forward. A real seeded evolutionary algorithm drives this; none of it is hard-coded animation.
  • Metrics per generation: completion time, estimated total cost, handoffs, bottleneck severity, resource utilization, constraint violations, and composite fitness.
  • Controls: replay, generation scrubber, compare two candidates, and show the actual scoring calculation and its assumptions.
  • Result: a floating recommendation card for the winning candidate with a Require Admin Approval action.

Insights /insights

  • An "intelligence observatory": flowing curves, timeline ribbons, heatmaps, topological views and a few sharp KPI readouts. Calm layout, lots of information.
  • Every chart has labelled axes with units, a source note, an empty state, and a visible uncertainty band where values are estimated.
  • Shows how collaboration opportunities, handoffs and bottlenecks change over time.

Finance /finance

  • Translucent budget jars filled like EcoMeters, allocation streams flowing between them, expense comparisons, and approval timelines.
  • Data comes from Capital One Nessie when configured. Sample accounts and simulated budgets are labelled. Includes the laptop-repair event's costs.

Personal Intelligence Sanctuary /profile

  • Your representative orb, with skills, goals, capabilities and interests orbiting it as glass tokens.
  • Every token has its own sharing level: Private, Approved connections, or Organization context. The level shows on the token itself as ring style plus an icon, not as color alone.
  • Also: consent history, pending requests, revoking permissions, data-source management, and "why does Wayfinder know this?" explanations.

Settings /settings

  • Theme (Day / Deep Ocean), motion, sound, data sources, and the status of each integration (Connected / Fixture / Not configured). An integration only shows Connected after a real check.

Every page

Each page needs designed empty states (a still glass scene with one clear next action), loading states (pearlescent shimmer skeletons in the shape of the real content), and error states (a coral droplet, plain-language cause, retry).

7. Motion and live behaviour

Springs by default: stiffness about 170, damping about 22, no visible overshoot on text. Idle motion is slow (3–8s cycles), and live events are fast and focused (300–800ms).

EventWhat moves
IdleNodes breathe on their own phases, particles drift along threads, background bubbles rise slowly
Pointer near a nodeNearby nodes lean slightly toward the pointer and the local cluster brightens (throttled)
Coordinated activityThreads on the active path light up aqua in sequence
Opportunity foundA bud appears, strands reach toward it, then it blooms into a flower
Consent requested / givenThe ConsentHalo shimmers, then closes into a mint ring
NotificationA toast shaped like a water droplet falls in from the top right, settles, and fades
CompletionA soft mint bloom and a single ripple. No confetti.
Route changeThe camera glides between rooms that sit next to each other in one world
Scroll (welcome)Gentle parallax between the sky, sphere and glass-pane layers
LoadingPearlescent shimmer skeleton in the shape of the real content
Sound (opt-in)Synthesized ambient tones and soft chimes on bloom and completion. Muted by default.
Reduced motionBreathing, particles, parallax and the camera glide stop. State changes become crossfades of 150ms or less. Everything stays usable.

9. Responsive, accessible, fast

Responsive

  • Design for 1440px and 1920px stage screens first. Tablets keep the canvas with larger touch targets.
  • Phones get an accessible 2D view of the network (clusters as a list plus a mini map) and full-screen inspectors as bottom sheets, not a shrunken desktop canvas. The AquaDock becomes a bottom pill that scrolls sideways.

Accessibility

  • Every state has an icon and a label as well as a color, and node types differ by silhouette.
  • Focus is a 2px Deep Azure ring with an aqua outer glow, which stays visible on glass. Canvas nodes can be reached with Tab and arrow keys through an HTML layer on top of the canvas.
  • Every canvas has a screen-reader description and a live region that announces stage and state changes.
  • Dialogs use accessible primitives (focus trap, Esc to close, returning focus to where it was).

Performance

  • Target 60 FPS on capable desktops. Use adaptive DPR and level of detail (LOD) for big graphs: skills and labels drop out first.
  • Pick the right layer for each job: WebGL (R3F) for the hero sphere and selected 3D scenes, Canvas for large networks, SVG for workflow threads, and semantic HTML for every overlay and all text.
  • Never give each animating node its own React state. Animate in the render loop or with refs, batch updates, and throttle pointer handling.
  • Fonts load through next/font, and art assets are compressed.

10. Honesty and data rules

  • Fixture badge: any view showing seeded or simulated data says so in the top bar, and the figures themselves are labelled.
  • Provenance: every connection, match and score can be inspected. A personal connection is never drawn without its source.
  • Message truth: messages go Simulated → Prepared → Sent, and Sent only after the backend confirms.
  • No auto-execution: consequential actions (money, introductions, acting on someone's behalf) always wait for authorization.
  • Integrations: Gemini, Photon, Nessie and the rest show Connected only after a live check. Otherwise they show Fixture or Not configured.
  • Estimates: Time Machine and Darwin outputs always show their assumptions and confidence, and are labelled Simulated.

11. Open questions

These are places where the prompt and the README don't line up, for the team to decide.

  1. Names: the prompt uses /discover, /orchestrate and /evolution. The README and v1 use Mycelium, Flows and Darwin. Should the engine names stay as labels in the UI, with the prompt's paths as the URLs?
  2. Scope: the README marks Time Machine / Lab as a stretch goal and Darwin as "simple". The prompt makes Darwin one of the top three and asks for a full Time Machine. Which wins for the 24-hour build?
  3. Amber hex: the prompt asks for amber on consent but doesn't define a token. Is #F5B83D okay?
  4. Night mode: is Deep Ocean mode needed for the demo, or is it a post-hackathon feature?
  5. Other sponsors: do Solana receipts, ElevenLabs voice and Snowflake/Cortex get any UI here, and if so, where?
  6. Projector check: the 14px minimum and the contrast tables should be tested on the real stage screen before the visuals are frozen.
